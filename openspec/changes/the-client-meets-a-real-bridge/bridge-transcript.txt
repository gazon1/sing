The bridge's own frames, as this application's client saw them.

Taken from `scripts/acp-bridge-probe.sh` against
`@agentclientprotocol/claude-agent-acp` 0.95.0, on 2026-10-24. The probe drives the
client and copies the bytes underneath the protocol's reader, so these are the frames
the bridge wrote, in the order it wrote them.

Two things are redacted, and both of them are the machine's rather than the bridge's:

  * the *model* configuration option carries the list of models the machine running the
    bridge is configured for. The option itself, its category, its type and its shape
    are here; the models it offers are not. Its list of choices is replaced by one
    placeholder of the same shape — the same keys, the values taken out — so that a
    reader comparing this frame with the fake's does not conclude that the fake is the
    one with the wrong shape;
  * the available commands are the machine's own catalogue — what the agent behind the
    bridge happens to have installed — and not a property of the protocol. The
    notification, its `sessionUpdate` kind and two of its commands are here, with a
    placeholder standing in for the sixty-seven that are not.

Neither redaction touches what the client has to read: the shape of the frames is the
whole point of this file, and the shape is complete.

What the frames show, and what the fake now sends:

  * the session's answer carries five permission modes and three configuration
    options — a mode, a model and an effort level;
  * the authentication state comes as a notification, during the handshake and before
    the session answer;
  * the available commands come as another notification, after the session is open —
    later than the session answer, which is why the probe reads the transcript over a
    window instead of at the moment the connect returns.
