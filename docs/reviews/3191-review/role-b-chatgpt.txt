### 2. Specific architectural checks

**Navigation saved state**

`Nav3SavedState.kt` is correctly located in `commonMain`.

The important distinction is preserved:

- Each navigation graph continues to receive its own `SavedStateConfiguration`.
- The new `navKeySaver()` creates its own configuration for serializing the selected top-level route.
- Serialization reuses `AppNavKey.serializer()` and `subclassesOfSealed`.
- `rememberSaveable` restores the selected route.
- `allowedRoutes` prevents restoring a route for which the shell has no back stack.
- Undecodable saved state falls back instead of crashing.

This does not reintroduce the defect documented in `2026-09-29-saved-state-config-must-be-per-graph.md`.

The older sealed-root ADR is also respected: there is still one sealed navigation-key hierarchy, rather than another manually maintained serializer.

**Nav3State**

The revised invariant is correct.

Previously, the selected route had to equal the start route. That invariant was incompatible with restoring a different selected tab.

The new invariant checks the actual requirement: the selected route must have a back stack.

The tests cover both cases:

- Restoring a route other than the start route.
- Rejecting a route without a corresponding back stack.

The implementation does not unnecessarily widen the navigation API.

**Orientation fixture**

The placement in `scripts/src/emulator/maestro/_fixtures.py` is appropriate.

The implementation separates three responsibilities:

1. `reset_orientation()` establishes the required device state.
2. `_take_rotation()` captures the original settings once per device.
3. `restore_orientation()` gives the settings back.

Registration in `_cli.py` is also correct.

The close callbacks execute in reverse registration order:

1. Restore orientation settings.
2. Turn off the screen.
3. Release the device lock.

This ensures restoration happens while the screen is still awake and while the runner still owns the device.

The tests cover restoration, ordering, unreadable settings, dead devices, repeated setup and repeated restoration.

The module-level map is justified by the one-device-per-process runner model.

**Qualification evidence**

The registries retain the established separation:

- `maestro-debt.yaml` describes known causes and their ownership.
- `maestro-flow-qualification.yaml` records measured evidence.

The new entries distinguish fixed causes from causes that did not reproduce.

Keeping the latter as accepted debt is reasonable: a future recurrence can be recognized without pretending that three successful attempts prove the original observation was impossible.

The qualification records also correctly identify the tested APK rather than attempting to identify themselves by the commit containing them.

**ADRs**

All three new decisions have the expected structure:

- Context
- Alternatives

They explain behavioural consequences rather than merely documenting implementation details.

### 3. Attachment helpers — explicit answer

<badge color="success">Not a finding</badge>

The removal of `centerElement: true` is justified.

The important distinction is between:

- Making an element visible.
- Making Maestro scroll until the element is centered.

The `attachments_add_file_row` is the last row in the sheet. Centering it causes Maestro to continue scrolling even after it is visible, eventually timing out.

The replacement is better:

- Ordinary scrolling stops when the row becomes visible.
- The tap uses `point: "50%,25%"`.
- The upper quarter of the row avoids the system gesture-navigation area.
- The tap does not depend on the amount of remaining scrollable content.

The two helpers have complementary responsibilities, and the implementation preserves them.

I would not restore centering or introduce another scrolling abstraction.
